## 1. Insurance imagery / nonrenewal laws

### 1.1 Louisiana — La. R.S. 22:1339 (enacted; live)

**Confidence: High.**

- **Statute:** La. R.S. 22:1339, added by Acts 2024, No. 151 (SB 242), **effective 22 May 2024**. Official text: [legis.la.gov Law.aspx?d=1389148](https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=1389148). Enrolled act: [legis.la.gov ViewDocument d=1379425](https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1379425). Digest: [d=1383557](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1383557).
- **Who it applies to:** An **insurer**, on a **policy of homeowners’ insurance**, for **cancellation or nonrenewal**. It does **not** mention claims handling, new-business underwriting, rating, or premium increases.
- **Operative language (A):**

  > “An insurer shall not solely rely upon aerial images of an insured property to identify the specific condition that serves as the insurer’s basis for cancellation or nonrenewal of a policy of homeowners’ insurance unless the images are taken within twenty-four months of the date of the cancellation or nonrenewal of the policy. All images, including aerial images, that are not utilized to identify the condition that serves as the basis for cancellation or nonrenewal or that are utilized solely for the purpose of identification and location of the immovable property and any improvements to the immovable property may be used without a limitation on the age of the photograph.”

- **What “aerial images” means (B):**

  > “pictures, recordings, or any physical evidence captured in the airspace above a property from an aircraft or other airborne platform, including but not limited to third-party proprietary aerial imagery taken by a fixed-wing aircraft and satellites.”

- **Penalties / private right of action:** None in § 1339. No DOI bulletin implementing it was found on this pass. Enforcement, if any, would have to ride on the general Insurance Code (unfair trade practices / commissioner powers), not a dedicated PRA. **Confidence: High that the section itself creates no PRA; Medium that DOI would treat a stale-image sole-reliance nonrenewal as an unfair practice.**
- **Checkable without dating a reroof?** **Yes, if the image date is known.** The audit is `image_capture_date` vs `cancellation_or_nonrenewal_effective_date` ≤ 24 months, **and** whether the insurer “solely relied” on that image for the condition. The location/identification carve-out is a defense the carrier will use. The agent cannot complete the audit if the carrier never discloses the image or its date.

### 1.3 Georgia — two different laws; only one is an imagery hook

**Confidence: High.**

#### A. Ordinary nonrenewal notice (not imagery)

- **Statute:** O.C.G.A. § 33-24-46(d)(1), amended by **SB 35**, signed 2025, **effective 1 January 2026**, applicable to policies issued/delivered/renewed on or after that date. [gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/sb-35/download](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/sb-35/download).
- **Operative change:** notice “shall not be less than **30 60** days from the date of mailing or delivery.” Reason for nonrenewal still required under Chapter 39.
- **This is ordinary nonrenewal timing**, not a special aerial-imagery rule. Many states have 30/45/60-day notice. Florida’s residential nonrenewal notice is already longer (Fla. Stat. § 627.4133). **Do not sell this as an imagery appeal right.**

#### B. Aerial/satellite imagery (the real Georgia hook)

- **Statute:** New **O.C.G.A. § 33-9-45**, added by **HB 1344** (Georgia Insurance Affordability and Claims Integrity Act), **Act 635, signed 12 May 2026, effective 1 January 2027**. Official enrolled text: [gov.georgia.gov/document/2026-signed-legislation/hb-1344/download](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2026-signed-legislation/hb-1344/download). Bill history: [legis.ga.gov/legislation/73286](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/73286).
- **Scope:** Admitted insurer; **owner-occupied residential** property; **cancellation or nonrenewal** that “relies on the use of aerial images.” Explicitly **not** placement in surplus lines or the FAIR Plan as the “adverse underwriting decision” definition — but subsection (c) then makes the section applicable to the FAIR Plan as it relates to residential properties.
- **Definitions that matter:**
  - `'Aerial images'` = photographs or other images, **except for satellite images**, captured from an aircraft, drone, balloon, or unmanned aerial system.
  - `'Satellite images'` = images captured from a satellite.
  - Both are regulated when used as a basis for cancellation/nonrenewal.
- **Operative duties (b)(1)–(5), paraphrased with quotes:**
  1. Notice of the adverse underwriting decision must include “copies, or instructions as to how to access copies, of the **date stamped** aerial images or satellite images” and “a description of the steps the owner occupied residential property owner may take to reverse” the decision, “including the specific standards that any repairs must adhere to.” Images “**shall have been taken within 12 months** of the issuance of the adverse underwriting decision.”
  2. Point of contact + process to submit proof of completed work. Owner-submitted images generally must be “of equal or greater resolution and quality.”
  3. Appeal process to correct errors or misunderstandings.
  4. “**minimum of 60 days to cure** the condition or conditions … even if the date to cure exceeds the nonrenewal notice period provided for in Code Section 33-24-46.”
  5. Offer renewal or rescission if the owner submits proof of cure.
- **Penalties / PRA:** None in § 33-9-45. Commissioner “shall promulgate rules and regulations.” No private right of action in the enrolled text.
- **Checkable without dating a reroof?** **Yes.** Image date vs 12-month window; presence/absence of date-stamped copies in the notice; whether a 60-day cure window was offered. This is the strongest *process* hook in the set — the carrier must hand over dated images.

### 1.5 Other 2024–2026 imagery / roof / AI rules (FL, CA, TX, plus the states that actually moved)

#### Indiana — enacted statute (High)

- **HEA 1260 / Public Law 86**, signed **4 March 2026**. Enrolled PDF: [iga.in.gov/.../HB1260.06.ENRS.pdf](https://iga.in.gov/pdf-documents/124/2026/house/bills/HB1260/HB1260.06.ENRS.pdf). Bill page: [iga.in.gov/legislative/2026/bills/house/1260/details](https://iga.in.gov/legislative/2026/bills/house/1260/details).
- Adds **IC 27-7-12-6.5**. Definition: `"aerial image" means an image of a named insured's property captured from an airborne platform.`
- When aerial images are **“the sole reason for nonrenewing a policy,”** the insurer shall: put in the nonrenewal notice how to request copies; “**Photos must have been taken within the past twenty-four (24) months**”; point of contact for proof of completed work; appeal process; **at least 60 days to cure**; offer renewal if cured (carrier may still nonrenew for an unrelated reason).
- Nonrenewal notice itself moves from 20 to **60 days** (IC 27-7-12-4).
- **Applicability trap:** section 4 as amended and section 6.5 apply to policies **issued, delivered, amended, or renewed on or after 1 January 2027** (even though the sections are “effective July 1, 2026”). Do not audit 2026 Indiana policies under 6.5.
- No PRA in 6.5. Department “shall adopt rules.”
- **Checkable:** yes, same pattern as LA/GA — image date + process, not roof-dating.

#### Kentucky — DOI bulletin, not a statute (High)

- **Bulletin 2026-01**, Commissioner Sharon P. Clark, **11 March 2026**, “Use of Satellite/Aerial Imagery as Basis for Cancellations, Nonrenewals, and Claim Denials.” Official PDF: [insurance.ky.gov/ppc/Documents/Bulletin%202026-01.pdf](https://insurance.ky.gov/ppc/Documents/Bulletin%202026-01.pdf). Listing: [insurance.ky.gov/ppc/new_bulletin.aspx](https://insurance.ky.gov/ppc/new_bulletin.aspx?bullid=1).
- The bulletin itself says it is **“not legally binding on either the Department or the reader.”** It interprets existing KRS 304.12-230 (unfair claims: reasonable investigation based on “all available information”) and KRS 304.20-320 / 304.20-340 (cancellation/nonrenewal).
- **Satellite images alone** “are insufficient to justify a cancellation or nonrenewal” and “cannot reasonably justify the denial of a property damage claim, standing alone.”
- **Aerial images** (drone/aircraft) may be used as a basis for cancellation, nonrenewal, **or claim denial** if (1) clear enough to visualize the specific noncompliant conditions, (2) accompanied by a written summary identifying those conditions, and (3) “contain a **date stamp showing they were taken within the last 12 months**.”
- Streaking/discoloration is not independently enough. “An insured is **entitled to review** all satellite and aerial images relied upon.”
- **This is the rare bulletin that also covers claims.** Checkable on image date (12 months) and on “satellite-only” — without dating a reroof.

#### Rhode Island — DOI bulletin (High)

- **Insurance Bulletin 2025-3**, RI Department of Business Regulation, **18 August 2025**. Official PDF: [dbr.ri.gov/.../INS_Insurance Bulletin 2025-3 Aerial Imaging.pdf](https://dbr.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur696/files/2025-08/INS_Insurance%20Bulletin%202025-3%20Aerial%20Imaging.pdf).
- Applies to “initial consideration of risks, the tiering of risks, and the non-renewal of policies using aerial imagery as well as the determination of causes and amounts of loss.”
- Images must provide a “clear, accurate, and current **(less than 15 months old)** view.” Low-res / blurry / ≥15 months “cannot alone justify a cancellation or nonrenewal or be utilized to determine a claim settlement.”
- Failure on the claims side “may constitute a violation of R.I. Gen Laws Chapter 9.1 Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act.”
- Sharing images with the homeowner before nonrenewal is labeled **“best practice,”** not a hard mandate.
- **Checkable:** 15-month age, for both underwriting and claims, if the image date is known.

#### Colorado — DOI bulletin (High for existence; Medium for full text)

- **Bulletin B-5.57**, adopted **16 March 2026** (Nearmap state tracker; UP hosted a copy). A copy of the bulletin text was retrieved via [uphelp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/New-Bulletin-5.57-Aerial-Imagery.pdf](https://uphelp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/New-Bulletin-5.57-Aerial-Imagery.pdf) and a parallel attorney-hosted PDF. Prefer the Division of Insurance original when accessible.

- For general underwriting and rating, “aerial imagery should be **no more than twelve (12) months old**.” For claims, “recent enough to capture post-loss conditions.”

#### Florida — roof-age statute, not an imagery statute (High)

- **Fla. Stat. § 627.7011(5)** (as amended through 2024, including ch. 2024-182): insurer “**may not refuse to issue or refuse to renew** a homeowner’s policy insuring a residential structure with a roof that is **less than 15 years old solely because of the age of the roof**.” For roofs ≥15 years, homeowner may obtain an inspection by an “authorized inspector”; if the roof has **≥5 years of useful life remaining**, the insurer may not refuse to issue/renew **solely because of roof age**. ## 2. Who actually pays / who acts

### 2.1 Public adjusters

**What they are (NAPIA, first-party):** A PA “represents the insured home or commercial property owner (the policyholder) to **prepare, present and settle a property insurance claim**.” They work “only for you – not the insurance company, not the roofing company, not the general contractor.” They prepare “estimates, inventories and other factual information that may be required to prove your loss” and make sure the claim “meets all of your insurance company’s filing requirements.” [napia.com/what-is-a-public-adjuster](https://www.napia.com/what-is-a-public-adjuster).

**Licensing:** NAPIA states PAs are licensed in 46 states; the four that “do not license public adjusters are: Alabama, Alaska, Arkansas, and South Dakota.” [napia.com/uppa](https://www.napia.com/uppa). Confirm per DOI; licensing lists move.

**What they file today:** A claim package — estimate (often Xactimate), inventories, photos, correspondence with the company adjuster. That is a **first-party property claim**, not a nonrenewal appeal. NAPIA’s own definition does not mention nonrenewal, underwriting, or DOI complaints as the PA’s core product.

**Fee caps (statute, not blogs):**

| State | Cap | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Florida | **10%** of claim payments for claims based on a Governor-declared emergency (for one year after the declaration); **20%** otherwise. Supplemental/reopened claims also capped at 20% of the *additional* payment. | Fla. Stat. § 626.854(11)(b). [flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/626.854](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/626.854) |
| Texas | Total commission “**may not exceed 10 percent** of the amount of the insurance settlement on the claim,” however calculated (hourly, flat, or percentage). | Tex. Ins. Code § 4102.104(a). [statutes.capitol.texas.gov IN.4102](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.4102.htm#4102.104) |
| Louisiana | **No percentage / contingent fee at all.** “A public adjuster shall not solicit for or enter into any contract … which provides for payment of a fee … contingent upon, or calculated as a percentage of, the amount of any claim … and any such contract shall be against public policy and is null and void.” Reasonable fee only. | La. R.S. 22:1703(A) |
| NCOIL model (not law) | Suggests 15% non-cat / 10% cat as a *ceiling states may undercut*. | [ncoil.org model April 2024](https://ncoil.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/NCOIL-Public-Adjuster-Model-April-2024.pdf) |

**Do PAs already buy EagleView / Pictometry / Hover?** No first-party EagleView, Nearmap, or Hover page was found that sells a **policyholder- or PA-branded** product. EagleView’s Feb 2025 Verisk integration is into **Xactimate / XactAnalysis** — tools used by carrier adjusters, independent adjusters, and restoration estimators. [eagleview.com press](https://www.eagleview.com/eagleview/eagleview-and-verisk-join-forces-to-streamline-property-insurance-claims/). PAs who already run Xactimate can order EagleView roof measurements **as Xactimate users**, not as a distinct PA SKU. That is a channel fact, not a “PA product exists.”

**Money on the table:** A PA is paid from **claim proceeds**. A nonrenewal produces **no settlement**. Unless the PA bills hourly/flat (required in Louisiana anyway), **nonrenewal work does not fit the dominant PA business model.**

### 2.2 Homeowners — private right of action vs DOI complaint

**Imagery statutes/bulletins reviewed (LA 22:1339, IN 27-7-12-6.5, GA 33-9-45, KY 2026-01, RI 2025-3, CO B-5.57) do not create a private right of action.** Enforcement language, where it exists, is commissioner rulemaking, unfair-claims statutes, or “the Department encourages.”

United Policyholders’ own consumer advice is: ask the insurer for the photos, try to reverse, **shop replacement coverage immediately**, and **“Report your situation to your state Department of Insurance.”** UP is explicit that in many states the practice is “technically legal” and that consumers currently lack a right to the images. [uphelp.org/aerial-photos-are-leading-to-insurance-non-renewals](https://uphelp.org/aerial-photos-are-leading-to-insurance-non-renewals/).

**Homeowners are the beneficiary, not the buyer.** Acquisition is one-shot, willingness-to-pay is low, and the remedy they are told to use is a DOI complaint plus a new policy — not a software subscription.

### 2.3 Roofers / restoration contractors — insurance-rebuild channel

**Official trade description of the fight, not a blog workflow:** The Restoration Industry Association publishes claims-handling position papers aimed at carriers, TPAs, and third-party consultants: duty to communicate with out-of-network contractors, timeliness, “adjusters dictating restoration prices,” “cost of doing business” denials, Xactimate-as-guideline not ceiling, undisputed sums. [restorationindustry.org/ria-position-paper-summary-guide](https://restorationindustry.org/ria-position-paper-summary-guide). That is a **claims/restoration** fight (scope and price after a loss), not a nonrenewal-imagery fight.

**UPPA wall:** NAPIA: representing or negotiating a property insurance claim without a PA or attorney license is the **unauthorized practice of public adjusting**. Contractors may repair; they may not “tell you what your policy does or does not cover,” “communicate directly with your insurance company about claim scope or payment,” or take a percentage of the settlement for “claim help.” [napia.com/uppa](https://www.napia.com/uppa). Texas makes the same line statutory: a contractor “may not act as a public adjuster or advertise to adjust claims for any property for which the contractor is providing or may provide contracting services” (Tex. Ins. Code § 4102.163); a PA “may not participate directly or indirectly in the reconstruction, repair, or restoration of damaged property that is the subject of a claim adjusted by the license holder” (§ 4102.158).

**Where roofers *do* fit this product:** Indiana and Georgia’s **60-day cure** windows turn a nonrenewal-for-roof-condition into a **repair job**. The contractor is a fulfillment channel after the packet identifies a curable condition. They are not the buyer of an appeal-dossier agent, and selling them a “we’ll fight the carrier” tool is how you walk into UPPA.

### 2.4 One primary buyer for v1

**Primary buyer: licensed independent property-casualty insurance agents (the producer of record on the homeowners policy that is being nonrenewed).**

Why that and not the others:

- They **already have the file** when the nonrenewal notice lands (Indiana even requires the insurer to notify the independent producer 10 days before mailing the insured — IC 27-7-12-4(b)).
- They are **paid on placement** (save the account with the same carrier after cure, or rewrite elsewhere). That is a check, not a hope.
- They can send a reconsideration package to the carrier **without being a public adjuster**, because they are the licensed producer on the risk (Texas even exempts a licensed P&C agent “while acting for an insured concerning a loss under a policy issued by that agent” from PA licensure — § 4102.051(b)(2) — and the nonrenewal fight is even further from “adjusting a claim”).
- The 30–60 day statutory notice **is their working clock**. The product has a deadline baked in.
- **Public adjusters** are the wrong primary buyer for *this* fight: their fee attaches to **claim settlements**, and the checkable statutes are **nonrenewal** statutes. They are the buyer of a later **claims** skin (KY/RI bulletins), not of v1.
- **Homeowners** are the user of the packet, not the person who writes a cheque for software.
- **Roofers** convert a 60-day cure into revenue; they must not negotiate coverage. Channel, not buyer.

### 5.2 ALTA 49 / 49.1

**Confidence: High.**

- Published **19 August 2025**. [ALTA press](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20250819-ALTA-Releases-Endorsements-to-Protect-Against-Forgery-Seller-Impersonation-Fraud). ## 6. Evidence / legal constraints on the “act”

### 6.1 Can EE / NAIP / Sentinel-2 “change dates” be exhibits?

**No official PA-association or DOI rule was found that blesses or bans Earth Engine chips as insurance-appeal exhibits.** Practice, from the sources that do exist:

- United Policyholders tells consumers to **ask for the carrier’s photos** and to correct misreads (skylight vs damage) with their own evidence. They do not mention Sentinel or NAIP. [uphelp.org](https://uphelp.org/aerial-photos-are-leading-to-insurance-non-renewals/).
- Georgia § 33-9-45(b)(2) lets the owner submit images, generally of “equal or greater resolution and quality” than the carrier’s. **10 m Sentinel-2 will lose that comparison against 7.5–15 cm Vexcel/EagleView.** NAIP at 0.3–0.6 m is in the fight; Sentinel-2 is a dating/context layer, not a substitute photo.
- Kentucky: insured “entitled to review” the carrier’s images; the Department may require the insurer to show “independent confirmation” of satellite-discovered issues. An independently dated NAIP chip is closer to “independent confirmation” of **vintage** than of **shingle condition**.
- Authenticity: USDA NAIP tiles carry acquisition metadata and are distributed by USGS EarthExplorer with a DOI (`10.5066/F7QN651G`). That is a cleaner chain of custody than a Google Maps screenshot. USGS EROS labels NAIP “Public Domain.” [usgs.gov EROS NAIP](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-aerial-photography-national-agriculture-imagery-program-naip).

**Build rule:** exhibits = **NAIP (and other public-domain / openly licensed government imagery) with acquisition date in the metadata**, plus a Mireye citation block. Sentinel-2 for “something changed between date A and date B,” labeled as 10 m and not as a roof-condition photo. Never as a secret substitute for the carrier’s own image, which the statutes want produced.

### 6.3 NAIP is public domain

**Confirmed.**

- USDA FSA Notice AP-26 (27 Nov 2017): “Since the start of NAIP in 2003, **all acquired imagery has been placed in public domain allowing unrestricted use and sharing of the data.**” [fsa.usda.gov/Internet/FSA_Notice/ap_26.pdf](https://www.fsa.usda.gov/Internet/FSA_Notice/ap_26.pdf). That cadence is **coarser than** LA’s 24-month and GA’s 12-month clocks — NAIP can **sometimes** prove the carrier’s image is older than a more recent NAIP flight; it cannot always supply a photo inside a 12-month window. Sentinel-2 (5-day) fills the dating gap at 10 m.

### 6.4 Unauthorized practice of law / unauthorized practice of public adjusting

**This is not legal advice. It is the line the owning regulators already drew.**

**Software may:**

- Assemble facts with citations (Mireye fields, NAIP date, statute text).
- Produce a **checklist**: image age vs statute; missing date stamp; missing copies; missing 60-day cure offer.
- Produce a **template letter** for a licensed human (agent, PA, or attorney) to send, clearly labeled as a draft.
- Produce a **DOI consumer-complaint packet** the **homeowner** files.
- Notify the producer of record that a nonrenewal notice appears to fail a named statutory element.

**Software / unlicensed operators may not:**

- “Prepare, complete, or file an insurance **claim**” or “act on behalf of, or aid an insured … in **negotiating for or effecting the settlement** of a claim … for money” — that **is** the definition of a public adjuster in Florida. Fla. Stat. § 626.854(1). [flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/626.854](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/626.854). The same section’s purpose clause is “to regulate public insurance adjusters and to **prevent the unauthorized practice of law**.”
- In Texas, act as a PA without a license (§ 4102.051); a PA license “**may not be construed as entitling a person who is not licensed by the Supreme Court of Texas to practice law**” (§ 4102.003); a license holder “**may not render services or perform acts that constitute the practice of law, including the giving of legal advice**” (§ 4102.156). [statutes.capitol.texas.gov IN.4102](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.4102.htm#4102.003).
- NAPIA UPPA: unlicensed parties may not interpret coverage, advise claim strategy, or negotiate with the insurer for a fee. [napia.com/uppa](https://www.napia.com/uppa).
- Auto-send an “appeal letter” in the product’s voice as if it were counsel. That is the UPL/UPPA tripwire.

**Product line to stay behind:** the agent emits a **dossier + draft + filing packet**. A licensed agent, PA, or attorney hits send. The software never holds itself out as representing the insured.

## 7. Objective

**What we build.** A jurisdiction-aware **nonrenewal imagery-audit agent**: given a US residential address and a cancellation/nonrenewal notice (and, when the carrier produced it, the image), it checks the image date and the notice’s process against the statute or bulletin that actually applies, attaches independently dated public-domain imagery and Mireye-cited parcel/hazard facts, and emits a producer-ready reconsideration packet plus a homeowner-ready DOI complaint draft.

**Buyer.** Licensed independent P&C insurance agents (producer of record). They already receive the notice, they are paid to keep or rewrite the account, and they can send the packet without becoming a public adjuster.

**The fight.** **Nonrenewal / cancellation**, not claims, not new-business underwriting.

**The checkable hook.**  
- Live now: **La. R.S. 22:1339** (no sole reliance on aerial/satellite images older than 24 months).  
- Live 1 Jan 2027: **IC 27-7-12-6.5** (24-month photos if sole reason for nonrenewal; copies-on-request; 60-day cure) and **O.C.G.A. § 33-9-45** (date-stamped copies in the notice; 12-month images; 60-day cure).  
- Bulletin overlay (KY 12-month + satellite-alone ban + right to review; RI 15-month including claims; CO 12-month underwriting): use as complaint ammunition, not as a fake statute.

**The satellite job.**  
- **Must prove:** an independently dated public image of *this parcel* (NAIP acquisition date; Sentinel-2 sensing date) and, when the carrier image is in hand, whether that carrier image is older than the statutory clock.  
- **Optional:** coarse change detection (roof color, tarp, vegetation) labeled with resolution limits.  
- **Out of scope for v1:** dating a reroof from space; scoring shingle condition against EagleView/Vexcel; Google Aerial View / Street View stills.

**The agent action.** Emit (1) a cited dossier, (2) a fill-in reconsideration / image-request letter for the licensed producer to send, (3) a DOI complaint packet for the named insured to file, (4) a cure-window calendar when IN/GA apply. Do not auto-file, do not negotiate, do not interpret coverage as counsel.

**Why not Title Pirate as v1.** FBI/IC3 documents the vacant-land impersonation scam but publishes **no** vacant-land share. ALTA’s 85% figure is a survey of target *type among companies that saw SIF*, not a loss census. **ALTA 49 does not cover vacant land** (1–4 family improved residential only). Title underwriters already buy recorded-document and identity tools (VeriTitle, notary/cash red flags); nobody is selling them a satellite occupancy fingerprint. There is no date-vs-statute hook. The physical trick can be detector #2 on the same skeleton **after** the insurance packet is real.
